# Security Champions KPI Framework

## BLUF

In the first 60 days, measure **trust and engagement.**

In the next 30 days, measure **capability transfer.**

After 90 days, measure **risk reduction and delivery impact.**

If trust is low, reduce scope.
If impact is low after 90 days, adjust the delivery model.

This approach ensures the program remains effective, credible, and aligned with engineering and business goals. Credibility between security and product teams is fragile — especially in organizations where previous security programs have failed or overpromised. Protect it by measuring the right things at the right time.

> **In Phase 1, the program optimizes for trust, participation, and capability formation — not raw vulnerability counts. Security impact metrics are expected to mature after sustained engagement.**

---

## Leading Indicators (First Quarter)

### Program Viability and Trust

*These indicators answer the question: Is this program safe, credible, and sustainable for this organization?*

#### Manager Trust and Buy-In

| Metric | Healthy Signal | Risk Signal |
|---|---|---|
| Manager approval rate | 100% | Below 90% |
| Invite acceptance rate | ≥ 60% | Below 40% |
| Manager qualitative feedback | Net positive | Workload or prioritization concerns |
| Time to manager approval | Under 1 week | Over 2 weeks |

**How to interpret:** Fast approvals and voluntary acceptance indicate the program is perceived as legitimate and valuable. Delays or resistance signal organizational or prioritization risk — not individual performance issues. Address resistance at the manager layer, not the champion layer.

---

#### Champion Engagement and Energy

| Metric | Healthy Signal | Risk Signal |
|---|---|---|
| 30-day active participation | ≥ 90% | Below 75% |
| Meetup participation | ≥ 80% | Below 60% |
| Collaboration activity | Organic peer interaction | Activity driven only by Security team |
| Early attrition | < 10% | Greater than 25% |

**How to interpret:** Strong early engagement indicates scope and expectations are appropriate. Low engagement suggests the program is too heavy or misaligned — adjust, don't intensify.

---

#### Capability Formation

| Metric | Healthy Signal | Risk Signal |
|---|---|---|
| Champions completing first triage task | ≥ 70% | Below 50% |
| Time to first meaningful contribution | Under 30 days | Over 45 days |
| Nature of champion questions | Application-focused | Foundational confusion persists |

**How to interpret:** These metrics confirm whether the program is successfully transferring practical capability rather than delivering abstract training. Champions asking "how do I apply this?" is a healthy signal. Champions still confused about basics after 30 days suggests a training or onboarding gap.

---

## Transitional Indicators (Later Q1)

*These indicators bridge participation to measurable impact.*

### Workload Absorption

| Metric | Healthy Signal |
|---|---|
| Low/medium findings triaged by champions | ≥ 40% |
| Central security team intervention required | Decreasing over time |
| Accuracy of champion triage | Improving over time |

**How to interpret:** At this stage, success means champions are confidently handling routine issues — not that fixes are perfect or comprehensive. Accuracy improving over time matters more than accuracy being high from day one.

---

### Knowledge Creation and Sharing

| Metric | Healthy Signal |
|---|---|
| SOPs drafted | At least one per champion |
| SOP reuse by non-champion teams | Organic adoption observed |
| Peer mentoring activity | Champions actively assisting other engineers |

**How to interpret:** This indicates the program is becoming self-reinforcing and capable of scaling beyond the initial cohort. Organic SOP adoption by non-champions is one of the strongest early signals of program health.

---

## Lagging Indicators (Q2+)

*These indicators determine whether the program should continue, expand, or be restructured.*

### Security Outcomes

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Vulnerabilities remediated on champion teams | ≥ 2x baseline rate |
| Mean time to remediation | ≥ 25% faster than pre-program |
| Late escalation of high-risk findings | Reduced quarter-over-quarter |

**How to interpret:** These metrics demonstrate tangible risk reduction and improved engineering throughput. Compare champion teams against non-champion teams as a control group where possible.

---

### Organizational Value and Adoption

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Champion retention after 90 days | ≥ 80% |
| Manager satisfaction | ≥ 70% net positive |
| Requests for additional champions | ≥ 25% of pilot organizations |
| Leadership references | Program mentioned in QBRs or planning forums |

**How to interpret:** When managers begin *requesting* additional champions rather than being asked to provide them, the program has shifted from push-based to pull-based adoption. This is one of the clearest signals of program maturity.

---

## Metrics We Intentionally Avoid in Early Phases

The following metrics are deliberately deprioritized during program launch. They create misleading incentives and obscure real signal.

| Metric | Reason |
|---|---|
| Training completion rates | Attendance does not equal capability |
| Total vulnerabilities fixed | Scale effects obscure meaningful signal |
| Champion hours logged | Incentivizes burnout over impact |
| Tool usage counts | Activity does not equal value |

**How to interpret:** The goal is to measure outcomes and sustainability — not effort or activity. Programs that optimize for these vanity metrics early tend to look healthy on paper while losing their best champions quietly.

---

## Decision Framework

Use this framework to determine when and how to adjust the program based on metric signals.

| Condition | Response |
|---|---|
| Manager approval rate drops below 90% | Investigate and address organizational resistance before expanding |
| Early attrition exceeds 25% | Reduce champion workload, revisit scope and expectations |
| Capability formation stalls past 45 days | Revisit training approach and task delegation quality |
| Impact metrics flat after Q2 | Adjust delivery model; consider restructuring champion responsibilities |
| Pull-based demand emerging | Accelerate expansion; begin Phase 2 planning |
