## Program Retrospective Template
 
**Cadence:** Bi-annual (every 6 months)
**Duration:** 90–120 minutes
**Participants:** All active champions, Security team, program owner
**Optional:** 1–2 managers or directors willing to provide stakeholder perspective
 
Retrospectives are how the program learns and adjusts. They are not performance reviews and not status updates. They are structured conversations about what is and is not working — led with honesty, documented with specificity, and followed with action.
 
---
 
### Pre-Retro Preparation (Security Team, 1 week before)
 
Send champions a short pre-read survey covering:
 
- What has gone well in the program this period?
- What has been frustrating, unclear, or too heavy?
- What is one thing you wish the Security team did differently?
- What is one thing you wish your manager did differently?
- On a scale of 1–5, how likely are you to continue as a champion for the next 6 months?
- What would make you more likely to stay?
Anonymize and aggregate results before the session. Share the summary with all participants as the opening frame.
 
---
 
### Agenda
 
**[0:00 – 0:10] Framing**
 
- This is a safe space — the goal is honest reflection, not performance
- Review the aggregated survey summary
- Set the expectation: every major theme gets a documented action item or a documented reason we are not acting on it
---
 
**[0:10 – 0:30] What Is Working**
 
Use a structured exercise. Ask champions to each share one thing that is working — something the program should protect or double down on.
 
Capture themes. Do not skip this section to spend more time on problems. Knowing what to protect is as important as knowing what to fix.
 
---
 
**[0:30 – 0:60] What Is Not Working**
 
Ask champions to share the most significant friction, confusion, or frustration from the past six months. Encourage specificity.
 
Categories to probe if discussion stalls:
- Workload and time protection
- Training quality and relevance
- Triage scope and tooling
- Recognition and visibility
- Relationship with the Security team
- Manager engagement and support
- Belt criteria and progression clarity
For each theme, ask: is this a program design problem, an execution problem, or an organizational problem? The response is different for each.
 
---
 
**[0:60 – 0:80] Course Correction Decisions**
 
For each significant theme, make a documented decision:
 
| Decision Type | Criteria |
|---|---|
| Act now | High impact, within program owner authority to fix, clear path to resolution |
| Act next cycle | Important but complex; plan it for the next six months |
| Escalate | Requires organizational authority above program owner (manager commitments, executive support, budget) |
| Accept and document | Real problem, no good solution available; name it honestly |
| Disagree with finding | Program owner and Security team disagree with the diagnosis; explain why |
 
Every decision gets documented. No theme should leave the retrospective without a disposition.
 
---
 
**[0:80 – 0:90] Appreciation Round**
 
End on this. Go around the group — each person names one champion or Security team member they want to specifically thank and why.
 
This is not optional. Programs that skip appreciation in retrospectives over time become programs where champions feel like they are doing thankless work.
 
---
 
**[0:90 – 1:00] Wrap-Up**
 
- Recap of action items and owners
- Next retrospective date
- Informal time for anyone who wants to stay
---
 
### Post-Retro (Security Team, within 1 week)
 
- [ ] Full notes published in champion channel
- [ ] Action items in tracking system with owners and dates
- [ ] Summary sent to managers and directors
- [ ] Executive summary sent to program sponsor (1 page: health signal, key themes, actions)
- [ ] Roadmap updated to reflect any phase or timeline adjustments
---
 
### Retrospective Health Signals
 
| Signal | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Champions share problems freely and without hedging | Psychological safety is present — this is a healthy program |
| Champions give only positive feedback | Either things are genuinely going well or people do not feel safe being honest — probe further |
| Same themes appear in consecutive retrospectives with no action | Execution problem or organizational blocker — escalate |
| Likelihood to continue score drops significantly | Retention risk — treat as urgent |
| Manager themes dominate | Manager accountability program needs attention before champion program can improve |
