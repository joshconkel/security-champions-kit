# The Value Proposition: What's In It For Everyone

## Why This Document Exists

Most Security Champions programs are designed as if people will participate out of goodwill, professional duty, or internal motivation alone. They won't — not sustainably.

This document exists because every stakeholder in the program has a different job, different pressures, different goals, and different reasons to say yes or no. If you cannot clearly articulate the value to each of them *in their own terms*, you will lose them — at recruitment, at renewal, or quietly over time when sprint pressure mounts.

**Use this document to:**
- Prepare for stakeholder conversations at each level before you have them
- Train your champions on how to make the case to their own managers
- Build your executive briefing and program charter arguments
- Anticipate objections before they become blockers

> These are broad strokes. Adjust the framing up or down depending on the individual. A VP who thinks like a Director or a Staff Engineer who thinks like a Manager will need a different conversation. Read the room.

---

## VP Level

*Priorities: Delivery risk, organizational scale, executive optics, durable change*

- Fewer late-cycle security findings that derail timelines, release dates, or features
- Scales security decision-making across the engineering org without scaling headcount
- Measurable reduction in delivery risk — pre-release vs. post-release findings, MTTR improvements — that makes the entire organization look good
- A durable mechanism to embed "security as quality" into engineering identity, reducing rework by default
- Clear executive-level reporting tied to business outcomes: rework avoided, incident reduction, delivery predictability

---

## Senior Director / Director

*Priorities: Predictable delivery, reduced escalations, cleaner accountability, team capability*

- Predictable delivery — fewer emergency security escalations in the final sprint before release
- A trusted local security lead in each area to reduce reliance on the central security team
- A structured path to build technical leadership within development teams, with visible career signal
- Reduced operational load from repeated findings — more time shipping features, less time fixing avoidable issues
- Cleaner accountability model: defined scope, escalation paths, and manager feedback loops

---

## Product Manager

*Priorities: Launch predictability, stakeholder reporting, cross-functional alignment*

- More predictable releases with fewer security surprises that impact launch dates
- Better risk tradeoff conversations with clear escalation paths and scoped ownership
- Evidence-based reporting to stakeholders: what changed, what risk was reduced, what was avoided
- Improved cross-functional alignment — security becomes part of delivery workflows, not a late-stage gate
- Champions who translate security requirements into actionable engineering work rather than abstract mandates

---

## People Manager

*Priorities: Sprint flow, team health, performance evidence, workload clarity*

- Less sprint time lost to churn and last-minute scrambles — better guidance earlier means fewer fire drills later
- Champions help the team move faster by preventing rework and improving PR quality before it becomes a problem
- Clear expectations and time bounds so the scope of champion work never becomes open-ended or threatening to delivery
- Recognition for them and their teams, with concrete evidence usable in performance reviews: PRs, SOPs, documented wins
- A safer way to support security improvement without becoming the security expert or depending on a central team to prioritize on their schedule

---

## Staff / Principal Engineer

*Priorities: Technical influence, scale, credibility, career framework alignment*

- Influence at scale: define secure patterns that many teams reuse and that ship into production
- Better engineering outcomes — quality, reliability — with fewer security incidents and escalations to manage
- A platform to mentor engineers and raise the bar through pods, threat modeling participation, and paved-road patterns
- Credibility demonstrating leadership activity directly aligned to architecture and technical staff responsibilities
- Less chaotic "security noise" because triage becomes structured and scoped rather than ad hoc

---

## Team Lead

*Priorities: PR flow, sprint predictability, reduced context switching, cleaner onboarding*

- Faster PR approvals and fewer blocked reviews because security expectations are clear from the start
- A local champion to lean on for triage and practical fixes — less back-and-forth with a central team on a different sprint cycle
- Better sprint planning with fewer surprise "security must-fix" tickets that break sprint flow
- Reusable checklists and SOPs that reduce context switching and make code reviews and threat models consistent
- Easier security onboarding for new engineers through standardized secure defaults and examples

---

## Senior Engineer

*Priorities: Practical guidance, signal-to-noise, skill growth, visible recognition*

- Clear, practical security guidance that helps you ship safer code with less guesswork
- Less time wasted on false positives — better signal-to-noise through triage help and SOPs
- Skill growth that maps directly to daily work: secure coding, PR review, threat modeling basics
- Recognition that is visible — shout-outs, spotlights, badges — and useful for career growth conversations
- Fewer production fires from avoidable security issues that should have been caught earlier

---

## Mid-Level Engineer

*Priorities: Concrete learning, faster feedback, community, a path forward*

- A concrete learning path that maps directly to daily work activities: PRs, fixes, patterns — not abstract training
- Faster feedback loops — catch issues earlier and avoid costly rework later
- More confidence navigating security scanner and linter results, and clarity on what to escalate versus fix
- Access to a community — channel, meetups, buddy system — so you are not learning or working alone
- A clear path into technical leadership if you want it, with visible progression criteria

## Common Objections and How to Address Them

| Objection | Who Says It | Response |
|---|---|---|
| "My team doesn't have time for this" | People Manager | The program is designed to reduce sprint disruption, not add to it. Champions prevent the fire drills, they don't create them. |
| "We tried this before and it faded out" | Director / VP | Previous programs failed because they were training-heavy and delegation-light. This framework delegates real work from Phase 1. |
| "How do I know this won't pull my best engineers away?" | People Manager | Manager buy-in is required and protected by design. Champions are nominated with your approval, not recruited around you. |
| "What happens when the champion leaves my team?" | Team Lead / Manager | Succession planning is built into Phase 3. The SOPs and patterns they create outlast any individual. |
| "How does this affect my performance review?" | Senior / Mid-level Engineer | Champion work is explicitly mapped to career framework criteria. Participation is documented, visible, and attributed. |
| "How do I explain this to my skip-level?" | People Manager | We provide reporting templates, talking points, and recognition materials designed for exactly this purpose. |
