# First 30 Days Guide
 
A structured week-by-week plan for new champions through the first month of their probation period. Share this alongside the welcome email or include it in the champion onboarding wiki.
 
---
 
## Week 1: Foundation
 
**Goal:** Get set up, oriented, and start building the habit of security thinking.
 
### Access and Setup
- [ ] Champion community channel access confirmed
- [ ] Scanner dashboard access confirmed (SAST, SCA, DAST for your team's scope)
- [ ] Training platform access confirmed
- [ ] Onboarding guide read
- [ ] Program charter read
- [ ] Roles and expectations document read
- [ ] Playbooks bookmarked
### Training
- [ ] Program orientation module completed
- [ ] Security fundamentals module started (CIA triad, common threat types)
### Connection
- [ ] Reached out to buddy and scheduled a 30-minute intro call
- [ ] Introduced yourself in the champion community channel
- [ ] 1:1 with Security team contact completed
### Reflection Prompt
At the end of week 1, spend 15 minutes answering this question in your notes:
 
*"Where in my team's current work do I think security is most likely to become a problem? What is one thing I noticed this week that I would not have noticed before joining the program?"*
 
You do not need to share this anywhere. It is for you. But writing it down accelerates your pattern recognition.
 
---
 
## Week 2: First Real Work
 
**Goal:** Complete foundational training and do your first triage with support.
 
### Training
- [ ] Security fundamentals module completed
- [ ] OWASP Top 10 — developer depth started
- [ ] SAST/SCA/DAST Triage 101 started
### First Triage
Your Security team contact will assign your first findings this week. Before you start:
 
- [ ] Read the relevant playbook (SAST Triage or Dependency Review)
- [ ] Review the triage decision framework
- [ ] Note any findings you are uncertain about before closing
For each finding you triage:
- [ ] Documented your disposition and rationale in full
- [ ] Flagged anything uncertain to your buddy or Security team contact before closing
- [ ] Did not close any finding without a written rationale
### Buddy Check-In
- [ ] Completed intro call with buddy
- [ ] Shared your first triage question or observation with buddy
---
 
## Week 3: Depth and Documentation
 
**Goal:** Complete core training and produce your first SOP.
 
### Training
- [ ] OWASP Top 10 — developer depth completed
- [ ] Secure coding basics in your primary language completed
- [ ] Security-focused code review basics started
### Triage Continued
- [ ] Continued triage of assigned findings
- [ ] Quality improving from week 2 — rationale is clearer, dispositions are faster
### First SOP: Start Drafting
By the end of this week, start drafting your first SOP based on something you triaged or fixed.
 
Your SOP should answer:
- What is the issue or pattern?
- How do you identify it?
- What do you do when you find it?
- What does a good fix look like?
- What do you escalate and when?
Length: 300–600 words. Short and usable beats long and ignored.
 
### Reflection Prompt
*"What is one thing about security I understood differently after triaging my first set of findings compared to what I expected going in?"*
 
---
 
## Week 4: First Meetup and SOP Completion
 
**Goal:** Attend your first meetup, complete your first SOP, and finish core training.
 
### Training
- [ ] Security-focused code review basics completed
- [ ] SAST/SCA/DAST Triage 101 completed
- [ ] All required Week 1–4 modules marked complete on training platform
### First SOP: Complete and Submit
- [ ] SOP drafted and self-reviewed
- [ ] Shared with buddy for a read-through
- [ ] Submitted to Security team contact for review and publication
### First Monthly Meetup
- [ ] Attended monthly champion meetup
- [ ] Introduced yourself if this is your first time with the full group
- [ ] Noted at least one thing from the meetup to follow up on
### 30-Day Check-In
At the end of week 4, send a brief update to your Security team contact covering:
 
- Training completion status
- Triage summary: how many findings reviewed, rough breakdown of dispositions
- One thing that went well
- One thing that was harder than expected
- Any access, tooling, or scope issues that need to be resolved
This is not an evaluation. It is a calibration. The program adjusts based on this feedback.
 
---
 
## Checkpoints: What You Should Have After 30 Days
 
| Area | Target |
|---|---|
| Training | All core modules complete or nearly complete |
| Triage | At least [X] findings triaged with documented rationale |
| Documentation | At least one SOP drafted and submitted |
| Engagement | Buddy connected; first meetup attended; active in champion channel |
| Relationship | Security team contact knows your name, team, and stack |
 
If you are significantly behind on any of these, flag it with your Security team contact. The 90-day review is easier if issues are caught and addressed at the 30-day mark.
 
---
 
## What Comes Next
 
At the 60-day mark, you will have an informal check-in with your Security team contact to assess progress and adjust if needed.
 
At the 90-day mark, you will have a formal probation review with both your Security team contact and your manager. Successful completion advances you to Green belt and comes with public recognition in the champion channel and quarterly spotlight.
 
Between now and then: show up, do the work, ask questions, and document what you learn. That is it.
